Four Practices

A few ways I spend my attention.

Security has taught me to look at problems from different angles. These are the areas I keep returning to — sometimes professionally, sometimes simply because I want to understand them better.

01
— Observation —

Defensive Monitoring

Understanding what is happening before deciding what to do about it. I spend a lot of time looking at logs, alerts, traffic, endpoints, and the small signals that become meaningful when viewed together.

  • Pace · Steady, sustained
  • Tools · SIEM, EDR, telemetry
  • For · People trying to understand their systems better
02
— Investigation —

Threat Intelligence

Following the thread beyond the alert. Threat intelligence, for me, is less about collecting indicators and more about understanding people, behaviour, infrastructure, and the patterns that connect them.

  • Pace · Slow, deliberate
  • Tools · OSINT, research, intelligence platforms
  • For · Analysts, researchers, curious minds
03
— Recovery —

Incident Response

When something goes wrong, the first job is to understand what happened. Incident response is where technical knowledge meets calm decision-making. Contain what needs containing, preserve what needs preserving, understand the evidence, then decide what comes next.

  • Pace · Deliberate, focused
  • Tools · IR playbooks, forensics, investigation
  • For · Engineers, security teams, leaders
04
— Foundation —

Secure Architecture

Security eventually becomes an architecture problem. Good security architecture isn't about creating the cleverest system. It is about creating systems that people can operate, understand, maintain, and defend for years.

  • Pace · Foundational, ongoing
  • Tools · Threat modeling, architecture review, security design
  • For · Architects, engineers, technical leaders

First Conversation

Bring one question. We'll see where it leads.

Sometimes you don't need a consultant. You just need someone who has seen a similar problem before. Bring a log you can't quite understand, an architecture you're unsure about, a security question that has been sitting with you, or simply an idea you want to think through. We'll spend 50 minutes looking at it together. No sales call. No pitch. No obligation.

  • 50 minutes, remote or in Jakarta
  • Bring one specific question, problem, or artifact
  • No preparation required
  • No homework
  • I read every message myself
  • If I don't know the answer, I'll say so
  • If someone else is better suited to help, I'll point you in their direction

You'll hear back personally. No newsletter, no follow-up sequence, no automation.
